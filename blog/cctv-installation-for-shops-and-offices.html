CCTV installation for shops and offices: what you actually need

Most of what gets written about CCTV procurement is aimed at large, multi-site rollouts. A single shop or office has a much shorter list of real decisions — and a much smaller budget to make them with. Here's what actually matters at that scale.

Start with what you're actually trying to see

A small shop or office rarely needs the camera count of a warehouse or campus — but it does need the right few cameras in the right places. Entry and exit points, the till or cash-handling area, stockroom access, and the parking or frontage outside are usually the four zones that matter most. Naming these upfront, rather than defaulting to "as many cameras as fit the budget," is what keeps a small installation useful instead of just decorative.

You don't need enterprise-grade storage or a server room

A single-location shop or office can run comfortably on a compact NVR with a couple of weeks to a month of recording retention — nowhere near the storage architecture a multi-site enterprise deployment needs. Oversizing this is one of the most common ways a small CCTV quote gets needlessly expensive; right-sizing it is one of the easiest ways to bring the cost down without cutting anything that actually matters.

Remote viewing matters more at this scale, not less

A large enterprise site usually has a dedicated security desk watching monitors. A small shop or office owner is the security desk — and is often not on-site all day. Reliable remote viewing from a phone, with alerts configured sensibly (not so aggressive that they get ignored, not so quiet that they miss something real), does more for a single-location business than any amount of extra camera resolution.

Certification still matters, even without a tender

STQC certification is usually discussed in the context of government and PSU tenders, where it's a formal requirement. For a private shop or office, there's no tender committee checking paperwork — but the underlying reason to care about it doesn't go away. STQC testing covers electrical safety, basic cybersecurity posture and conformity to spec — all things worth having whether or not anyone's asking to see the certificate. Every brand in our surveillance line-up — CP Plus, Honeywell, PRAMA, UNV and RoyalShield — is STQC certified regardless of the size of the job.

Plan for maintenance from day one, not after something breaks

A camera that's been quietly offline for three weeks is worse than no camera at all — it creates a false sense of coverage. An AMC (annual maintenance contract) with a defined check-in cadence is a small, predictable cost against a much bigger one: discovering the footage you needed was never being recorded in the first place.
